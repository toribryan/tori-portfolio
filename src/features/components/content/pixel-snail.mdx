A one-color pixel-art snail who crawls on a loop while something loads. He
used to be fibo's mascot and now he's its loader: slow, steady and happy to
wait with you. As a sprite in your own drawing, he can also build himself up,
dance and turn to watch the pointer.

<Exhibit

  description="The loader at its default size and pace, crawling in place while the ground slides under him."

<ExhibitCode label="Install">

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add https://fibo.toribryan.com/r/pixel-snail.json
```

It installs `PixelSnail`, `PixelSnailSprite` and the `PixelSnailLook` type.
There's no animation library: every frame is state and a timer, drawn as SVG
rectangles.

</ExhibitCode>
<ExhibitCode label="Usage">

```tsx
import { PixelSnail } from "@/components/ui/pixel-snail"

export function ProjectsLoading() {
  return <PixelSnail label="Loading your projects" />
}
```

`PixelSnail` is the loading indicator. `PixelSnailSprite` is the snail without
a frame: a bare `<g>` for placing inside your own SVG. Its origin is under the
middle of his foot, so a `transform` that moves him to a point stands him on
it.

```tsx
import { PixelSnailSprite } from "@/components/ui/pixel-snail"

export function SnailOnALine() {
  return (
    <svg viewBox="0 0 200 40" className="text-foreground" aria-hidden>
      <line x1={0} y1={30} x2={200} y2={30} stroke="currentColor" />
      <PixelSnailSprite
        transform="translate(100 30)"
        pixel={1.5}
        mode="dance"
      />
    </svg>
  )
}
```

Give every loader a `label` that says what's loading. It's what screen readers
announce.

</ExhibitCode>

## Anatomy

The loader is a status region holding one drawing: the snail, and under him
the dotted ground. The snail is shell, foot, head and a googly eye on each
stalk, all in one color. The sprite is the same snail on his own, with no
region and no ground.

## Variants and states

Everything he does follows three rules. He lives on a grid: he's drawn in
whole art pixels and only ever moves by whole art pixels, so he never blurs
between two. He's timed by hand: each loop is a script of frames and holds,
deliberately uneven, because an even beat reads as a machine. And he has one
color: he draws in the text color, so recoloring text recolors him.

### The loader

A crawl cycle has four frames. The head reaches forward first while the eye
stalks tip ahead, then the shell slides up while the tail holds back, so his
foot stretches and gathers instead of sliding. Each cycle moves him two art
pixels.

### Sprite modes

The sprite has two more loops besides the crawl. Rest runs about eleven
seconds, with holds from 140 ms to 1.8 seconds: he looks about, reaches,
blinks and shuffles a pixel forward and back, so he never ends a gesture where
it began. Dance is a beat you could count: his stalks sway every 280 ms while
his shell hops a row, three times over, then he shuffles aside and back and
takes a 1.6 second breather so it never turns frantic.

### Build-up

The sprite can load in like an image over a slow connection rather than
fading in:

- **0 to 400 ms:** 4-pixel blocks arrive in a scattered but fixed order.
- **400 to 520 ms:** the full coarse silhouette holds.
- **520 to 680 ms:** the blocks halve to 2 pixels.
- **680 ms:** the real art appears, eyes and all.

Each stage comes from the time since the delay ended, so a throttled
background tab skips ahead instead of dragging the build-up out. Every step is
reported once, for syncing sound to the pixels: a crunchy blip that climbs in
pitch as more of him arrives, a brighter one as the blocks split, and a chord
when he's complete.

<Exhibit
  of="pixel-snail.BuildUp"
  title="Build-up"
  description="Starts after 300 ms. The line under him shows each step as it's reported."
>
<ExhibitCode>

```tsx
<PixelSnailSprite
  mode="rest"
  assembleDelay={300}
  onAssemble={(step) => playBlip(step)}
/>
```

</ExhibitCode>
</Exhibit>

<Tip>
  Keep sound optional and quiet. The build-up reports its steps so you can sync
  it, but on most pages the snail should be seen and not heard.
</Tip>

### Looking

Looking at something stops whatever loop is playing and holds him in a pose.
Each direction has three settings, because pixel art can't show a finer angle.
Ahead or behind moves his pupils, leans his stalks and reaches his head
forward or back a pixel. Up raises his eyes on longer stalks and stretches his
neck; down sinks his eyes and ducks his head. Facing turns the whole snail
round.

### Watching the pointer

The sprite doesn't read the pointer himself: you decide what he looks at and
when, so he can just as well follow a caret or watch a drop target. To follow
the pointer, leave him a little slack. He glances only once the pointer is
more than 1.5 art pixels off his eye line, so his pupils don't flicker as it
crosses the middle, and he turns round only once it's more than 4 art pixels
past his middle, so he doesn't flip back and forth when you hover near him.

<Exhibit
  of="pixel-snail.FollowsThePointer"
  description="Move the pointer around the box. He dances again when it leaves."
>
<ExhibitCode>

```tsx
const EYES = { x: 6, y: -11 } // his eyes, from the sprite's origin
const GLANCE = 1.5
const TURN = 4

const glance = (offset: number) =>
  offset < -GLANCE ? -1 : offset > GLANCE ? 1 : 0

// dx and dy: the pointer in art pixels from the sprite's origin
const facing = dx < -TURN ? -1 : dx > TURN ? 1 : previous.facing
const look = {
  facing,
  x: glance((dx - facing * EYES.x) * facing),
  y: glance(dy - EYES.y),
}
```

</ExhibitCode>
</Exhibit>

## Accessibility

There's nothing to press: he isn't focusable and never needs to be. What
matters is what he says and how he moves.
