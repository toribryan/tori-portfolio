## Coverage ran on trust

Proctorio proctors LMS assessment sessions using both internal proctors and
third-party providers. Neither instructors nor providers could see how a proctor
actually spent a session: where their attention landed, or whether their device
and network held up throughout.

So coverage was a conversation about trust. Instructors assumed a proctor was
watching, proctors ran on the honor system, and when someone asked whether a
session had really been covered, nobody could answer with anything but a
shrug.

## The rules I set first

- Evidence over surveillance: build proof, not a live monitor.
- Anonymized by default, resolvable on demand.
- Zero new UI for students, no matter what.
- Accessible from day one, not retrofitted.

## The swimlane timeline

An analytics surface embedded directly in the instructor's review dashboard: a
swimlane timeline of every proctor in a session, with glanceable markers for
coverage gaps, connection degradation, and device issues. Identity is
anonymized by default but resolvable when an escalation requires it, and the
whole surface is keyboard accessible.

![The swimlane session timeline embedded in the instructor's review dashboard](/case-studies/proctor-coverage-segs-popovers.svg)

## What's in it

- A swimlane session timeline as the core evidence pattern
- Status markers distinguishing coverage gaps, connection degradation, and
  device issues
- A scaling control for zooming into a specific window of the session
- An anonymized identity chip that resolves to a real proctor on demand

![Coverage gap and device-status markers, zoomed into a session window](/case-studies/proctor-coverage-popovers.svg)

## The forks

**Evidence, not a live console.** The first fork was the one that mattered. A
live monitoring view was the obvious build, and it was wrong: instructors did
not want another screen to watch during an exam. They wanted to answer a
question after the fact, usually because a student had disputed something. That
reframing set the rest of the product. A timeline is proof; a feed is a chore.

**Embedded, not standalone.** Coverage evidence belongs where instructors
already go to review a session. A separate tool would have meant a separate
login, a separate habit, and a surface nobody opens until something has already
gone wrong.

**Anonymized but resolvable, decided early.** This is the detail that earned
trust on both sides. Proctors are not comfortable being individually scored on a
dashboard, and instructors cannot escalate against a number. Naming it as a
constraint at the start meant the identity model was designed rather than
negotiated late.

**Accessible from the first prototype.** Keyboard paths and ARIA semantics were
part of the design, not a remediation pass. A timeline is exactly the kind of
dense, spatial interface that becomes unusable without them, and exactly the
kind that is expensive to retrofit.

## In pilot

In pilot. The surface ships with zero added UI for students, which was the
hard constraint throughout, and it is being measured on three things: whether
coverage gaps are actually found, whether instructors report more confidence in
a session, and whether the interface holds up under keyboard-only use.

## What surprised me

- The strongest move was refusing the obvious build. A live feed would have
  shipped faster and answered nobody's question.
- Constraints concentrate value. Zero student UI meant every design decision had
  to earn its place on the instructor surface, which made that surface better
  than it would have been with more room.
- Trust is a design problem with a specific shape. "Anonymized by default,
  resolvable on demand" is a sentence, but it decided the data model, the
  interaction, and whether providers would agree to be measured at all.

## What I'd do next

- Pilot the anonymized-but-resolvable identity model with proctor providers
  directly, rather than only with instructors.
- Explore a lightweight coverage summary an instructor could share with a
  student who disputes a session.
- Test whether the swimlane pattern survives multi-day and multi-session exams,
  where the timeline stops fitting on one screen.
