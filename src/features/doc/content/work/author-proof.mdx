## Where it sits

Proctorio's integrity suite is three products: Integrity proctors the exam,
Vault protects its content, and Origin proves authorship. I led design across
all three. AuthorProof is Origin's product, and I took it from an assigned brief
to a shippable MVP.

<ProctorioSuites caption="The three suites. Hover one to see it move." />

## Did you write this?

Existing integrity tools ask whether a paper is original. A paraphrase, an AI
rewrite or a ghostwriter clears every one of them. None ask what instructors
want to know: did you write this?

<IntegrityRadar caption="What the suite has to cover. Authorship, in green, was the one no existing tool touched." />

AuthorProof asks it directly. When a student submits written work, it builds a
short quiz from that writing, in the language it was written in. Someone who
wrote the paper can answer questions about it; someone who didn't, can't. The
score appears beside the submission, where instructors already grade.

## The brief as it arrived

The brief came with its architecture chosen: three journeys built on the LMS's
own quiz tool, one quiz per submission, and a notification telling the student
to go find it.

<BriefedJourneys caption="The first concept as it was handed over, one journey per tab." />

Before engineering spent time on it, I mapped the flow step by step and timed it
against what the platform could do.

## Four constraints that moved the product

<Numbered>
  <Item title="The quiz tool is one-to-many.">
    It expects one quiz written once for a whole class. A new quiz per
    submission fights that at every step, and breaks when a class submits at
    once.
  </Item>
  <Item title="Ten minutes is enough to cheat.">
    Fetching, generating, creating, assigning and notifying took about ten
    minutes. That is all the time a student needs to reread their essay.
  </Item>
  <Item title="Students turn notifications off.">
    The fallback was instructors reminding students, which loads the work onto
    the person we were trying to help.
  </Item>
  <Item title="Two things to grade.">
    A separate quiz would sit apart from the submission, with no single view for
    the instructor.
  </Item>
</Numbered>

Each one changed where the product should live, not how a screen looked, so the
answer was a different flow rather than four workarounds.

## A smaller flow that kept the promise

Instead of reporting the blockers, I pitched an alternative, split into an MVP
and a longer vision. The MVP puts AuthorProof inside the assignment: the student
uploads their essay and the quiz appears seconds later.

<PivotFlows caption="The student's path as it was briefed, and as I pitched it instead." />

<Sides>
<Side label="What it fixed" title="One move, three constraints">

- The ten-minute gap closed to seconds
- No notification to miss
- Out of the quiz tool, with the result written back beside the submission

</Side>
<Side label="What it cost" title="Named up front">

- Short pieces written in the LMS editor were left out
- How proctoring would work alongside it was still open
- I flagged both before engineering could find them

</Side>
</Sides>

The longer vision went on the roadmap instead of being dropped, and Proctorio
later adopted it as the product's long-term direction, with the briefed idea
shipping first as the proof of concept.

## Confidence, not verdict

The result had to support a conversation between an instructor and a student,
never judge the student on its own.

<Principles>
  <Principle title="No pass or fail">
    A score ships with a confidence level, and the instructor decides what to do
    with it.
  </Principle>
  <Principle title="Show what was asked">
    Every question sits beside the words that prompted it and the assessment of
    the answer.
  </Principle>
  <Principle title="Never color alone">
    The host platform colors the badge the opposite way from the score, so the
    score, label and confidence always appear as text.
  </Principle>
</Principles>

## The prototype was the spec

I led design end to end, from concept to a production-ready MVP in four to six
weeks. I designed the quiz settings, the report card, the results and the
student emails, and built the front end for all of it in HTML, CSS and vanilla
JavaScript on our design system. Engineering got working prototypes instead of
a document about them.

## What I'd do next

<Numbered>
  <Item title="Test it with proctoring sooner.">
    Prototype the embedded flow beside a proctored session before the direction
    decision, not after.
  </Item>
  <Item title="Let instructors review questions.">
    Before a generated quiz reaches a student.
  </Item>
  <Item title="Pick the quiz language.">
    Beyond the default of the submission's language.
  </Item>
</Numbered>
